Raspberries are a status symbol food. 6&nbsp;ounces of organic raspberries at
Whole Foods will set you back $4.99. Three-eighths of a pound of food cost the
same as a week's worth of beans and rice dinners.

When I was growing up, my family would hike in the Colorado mountains, where
wild raspberries were a rare occasion for which the whole hike would be paused.
_Rubus strigosus_, a subspecies dwarfed by grocery store cultivars, usually
produced less pieces of fruit than there were members of my family, so berries
would be halved for distribution. Half a raspberry was a food to be savored
slowly; another might not be found for the rest of the trip. Their evanescence
made them special: spotted one moment, consumed the next, but remembered the
whole day.

I have an internship this summer, and I start every day at the office
refrigerator. My employer, ever focused on retention, keeps the fridges stocked
daily with Greek yogurt and raspberries, which I combine with fancy granola and
real maple syrup. But one morning, as the refrigerator door swung open, I
hesitated. Something wasn't normal. There I stood, frozen in the fridge light's
blue glow, thinking twice about what to eat.

Could it really be? Was I tired of raspberries?

How did I get here? I followed the usual American middle-class agenda: finish
grade school, earn a college degree, and use it to make a living. A mishmash of
privilege, luck, and genuine hard work kept me on the script. [I played the
College Board's game in high school](/blog/dear-high-schoolers), loaded myself
up with AP credits and a high SAT score, and got into a STEM university. Now,
with one semester of my computer science degree left, I'm interning at a
software company. Every box is checked. Clearly, though, the checklist isn't
enough, because at 21 years old, I am already tired of raspberries. It is a launchpad, not a rocket. (?) I don't like that last sentence

The luxuries this lifestyle provides can only feed me for so long. I hunger for
a different kind of fruit, one whose sweetness never fades and whose glory never diminishes. I need to figure out
what I want to do with my life.
